Callisto was a smooth grey ball because its surface map was missing. It now has its own crater mosaic, and nine other moons gain source imagery too. Dust impacts also leave short curved marks across the bubble wall, while the navigation chart gains a clearer density view.

## Callisto has a surface

![Callisto with its pinned USGS/NASA mosaic](https://storage.googleapis.com/stapledons-voyage-assets/site/064a636d4ca437425e693eaaeff7a1ab2a27f95f50c5c8444904516cd0fefd96.png)

This is the native planet renderer, staged at six Callisto radii for a fixed-exposure comparison. The **USGS/NASA monochrome mosaic** resolves craters and the bright scars around impact basins. Its mean brightness is normalized to the body's adopted visible albedo.

The added maps cover Callisto, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Mimas, Enceladus, Tethys, Dione, Rhea and Triton. Export checks now require all nineteen declared textured bodies, closing a loophole that only checked the original nine.

Most new mosaics are monochrome; Io and Triton use enhanced or filter-composite colour. Source seams and missing coverage remain visible, including Triton's unmapped north. Titan's surface maps use radar or infrared beneath its haze, so they are not used as ordinary visible-light terrain. [Image sources and credits](/docs/credits).

## Marks attached to the wall

![The same seeded flight with surface marks at a 60-degree field of view](https://storage.googleapis.com/stapledons-voyage-assets/site/309b9c0e553c8be45a1f769871b0aeee6060e117ea618d20b1cb4c6023011e9c.png)

The impacts retain their frequency, energy, temperature and decay. Their wall patches now stretch along incidence and bend slightly, preserving the original emitting area. They are still **G-AG, the labelled wall-afterglow approximation**. They do not claim simulated particle trails or lateral scattering.

![A magnified three-degree view of one of those same impacts](https://storage.googleapis.com/stapledons-voyage-assets/site/cd710f7923bf4d329caa6187a4cd7d058927e6c5f4f6807984485009dc644acc.png)

The second image magnifies one of the same seeded events to a three-degree field of view. CPU and GPU checks cover normal, glancing and off-centre views, overlaps and the integrated light budget.

## Clouds as instrument data

![The native navigation station with false-colour density shading](https://storage.googleapis.com/stapledons-voyage-assets/site/633ada3f53aa1d5dab52163ea1a2476ab3fce81700695bbda5a0d48f8c6ca2bd.png)

Press **D** or use **Interstellar medium**, then **Local ISM**, to see the local gas. **Density shading** fills the adopted LIC surface and published warm-cloud angular outlines at their approximate depths. Construction spokes are optional under **Depth guides**; labels separate where they would overlap. The colour scale describes gas density.

These nearby clouds are extremely tenuous and emit little visible light. Astronomers infer them from absorption in starlight; the hot gas of the Local Bubble is associated with soft X-rays. The chart is a false-colour instrument view, and flight keeps the impact-driven wall light. We have not added eye-visible nebular fog. [NASA's local-ISM description](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap020210.html), [NASA's Local Bubble explanation](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/nasa-funded-x-ray-instrument-settles-interstellar-debate/).
