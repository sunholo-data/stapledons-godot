Every Solar System leg now has the same rhythm, about 2¼ minutes in real time: 30 seconds accelerating, about a minute cruising, then braking into a 25-second final approach. The longer legs simply go faster, above 0.999c to Jupiter and Saturn, so the planets now ease into view instead of appearing in the last few seconds.

## What's new

- **Even legs.** The simulation solves each leg's thrust and cruise speed so the boost lasts 30 s and the cruise about 60 s: 0.07c for the short Callisto hop, 0.99943c (γ 30) to Jupiter, 0.99963c (γ 37) to Saturn. Sun → Jupiter takes about 2¼ minutes instead of 6½.
- **A gentle final approach.** A new trip profile brakes hard, then eases in for 25 s from where the planet is 4° across, so Jupiter grows smoothly instead of exploding in the last 8 s. It's built from the same exact constant-acceleration physics, in `sunholo/relativity` 0.9.0. The HUD says FINAL APPROACH.
- **Skip stage [K].** Jump to the next stage of the leg: accelerating → cruise → braking → final approach → arrival. It lands exactly on each stage, and the HUD counts how many you skipped.
- **Distances on the HUD:** to the destination, from the last stop, and from Earth, plus this leg's thrust.

## Not yet

At the faster in-system speeds, the bubble wall's glow tints the forward view; how to present it is open. Stars beyond the Sun still appear as points: giving every visited star its real size (TRAPPIST-1, Aldebaran) is the next sprint.
