This is the shared review reference for the chosen bubble ship: **seven major layers**
inside a sphere of radius 100 m, with **at least 5 m clearance for the lower-floor geometry**.
Mark chose this layout on 4 October 2026. The bridge uses its actual geometry, unchanged
in scale. Buildings, forests and other architecture will occupy the major tiers.

## The chosen geometry

The bridge floor is at **Z +82 m**, its main circular deck is **44 m across**, and the
actual needle reaches **Z +98 m**. The bubble is **200 m across**, centred at the ship
origin. Blender +Z is forward/up; −Z is aft/down. There is no occupied level above the
bridge. The three thin sphere circles are measurement guides, not a solid hull.

The seven-tier schedule follows a 25 m cadence: bridge, then +57, +32, +7, −18, −43 and
−68 m. Revision 2 replaces the old 55% radius rule with a **95 m radius inner spherical envelope**.
This gives every lower floor at least **5 m clearance to the 100 m bubble**, rather than a
gap that grows toward the equator. Deck widths are derived from the sphere, not the illustration.
The 1 m slab thickness and small edge lip are included in the fit:
`radius = sqrt(95² − max(Z², (Z−1)²)) − 0.08` metres.

The actual bridge is a deliberate exception in this study: its 22 m radius is much smaller
than the bubble cross-section at +82 m. It remains the existing decision platform above the
wider tiers; an expanded upper terrace would require a separate geometry choice.

Seven layers are major inhabited tiers, not seven single-storey floors. The 25 m cadence
leaves about 24 m between lower floor slabs before architecture and bracing. Buildings can
contain smaller storeys, and forests can occupy tall open volumes. The actual bridge ramp
and underside locally reduce the space beneath the bridge.

The lower floors are opaque slabs 1 m thick, with a tapered central spire beneath the
actual bridge. This is a **dimension and occlusion blockout**, not a structurally engineered
ship. Rooms, roofs, gardens, lifts, bracing and railings on lower levels are absent; they
will add occlusion. The 5 m clearance is for floor geometry only: roofs and tree canopies
need their own sphere checks at their actual heights. For a point at height `h`, its horizontal
radius must stay below `sqrt(100² − h²)`; using `sqrt(95² − h²)` also preserves the accepted
5 m buffer. Upper-tier buildings and trees need greater setbacks because the sphere narrows
overhead. The imported bridge rails and fronds are present. Invisible game
collision meshes are excluded. Bridge fronds have their existing attachments; speculative
lower-level growth has not been invented to fill the foreground.

## What you can see looking downward

From the interior eye, 4.11 m inside the edge, the centre ray at 30°, 45° and 60° down
hits the bridge floor. A camera cannot see through that floor.
With the revised wider floors, at the rim eye, 0.7 m inside the edge, the 30°, 45° and
60° centre rays reach the next deck. The 15° centre ray still clears the modeled ship. Use the angle selector
above to compare the full images: a centre-ray result describes only the middle pixel,
not everything in the wide field of view.

These are ordinary geometric visibility studies. They do not simulate relativistic
aberration or boundary glow. They establish which physical structures are in a camera's
view before those optical effects are applied.

## How this relates to the game panorama

The current game composite uses two different projections: the panorama/sky camera looks
inward and about 53.6° upward, while the orthographic play layer looks downward at 14°.
Those layers are not a single physically coherent camera for arbitrary tilting and panning.
The measured views here instead render **all ship geometry through one perspective camera**.
They are a reference for choosing future camera behaviour, not a claim that the current
composite already produces these sightlines.

Play, foreground and panorama describe rendering distance and composition, not physical
ship storeys. Nearby rails and fronds can form foreground parallax, but every visible
member needs a real root and a position in the shared model. The central spire is one
continuous structure; it must not be duplicated in a panorama plate.

## From the Blender reference to the game

**Recommended approach:** keep one master ship assembly in metres, with linked collections
for the bridge, six lower tiers, central structure, architecture and vegetation. Export
separate game assets from that common design, so all views share positions and proportions.

| View | Asset derived from the shared design |
|---|---|
| Walking and close inspection | Detailed bridge or local tier sections, with nearby architecture and gameplay collision |
| Looking down or pulling back to the whole ship | Simpler tier and exterior meshes, with reduced detail for distant buildings and forests |
| Galaxy map | Small ship image or simplified mesh, kept legible as a schematic marker |

The bridge can be the first detailed section of this assembly. The lower tiers become
part of the same coordinate system rather than unrelated scenery. For a continuous
zoom and tilt, visible ship geometry needs consistent camera projection. The current
painted bridge plate is composed for its fixed isometric view; extending free camera
movement will also require suitable materials or baked textures for the other angles.

The current game loads an area's GLB from an area bundle at runtime. The galaxy map
currently draws a diamond at the simulated ship position. Neither a detailed whole-ship
loader nor a model-based map marker is implemented by this reference-page change.

A smaller transform alone does not make a fully detailed ship inexpensive. Use reduced
geometry and materials for distant views, and keep full local detail to the nearby areas.
Godot offers [mesh LOD](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/mesh_lod.html)
and [visibility ranges](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.html)
for detail changes. Its automatic LOD is an import-time facility; the current runtime
GLB loading path needs deliberately prepared detail levels or a revised import pipeline.

At interstellar map scale, a physically sized 200 m ship is invisible. A map marker
should therefore keep a readable screen size, anchored to the true simulated position.
A rendered ship icon can share the ship's silhouette and colours without loading its
interior. A selected-ship close-up can show the simplified 3D model.

## One viewpoint across the ship and stars

The approved demo uses **one logical observer**: eye position, forward/up, perspective field
of view and viewport size. Nearby decks, rails, architecture and real foreground members
use that same view. There are no independently drifting copies in a panorama plate.

The sky may be rendered in a separate pass so stars stay behind solid ship geometry, but
its viewing rays must agree with the ship camera. Separate render passes do not imply separate
viewpoints. Camera tilt, roll, field-of-view changes and resize must update both together.
The bubble glow also needs the current eye position when the captain or lift moves.

The existing SR renderer changes external light using the simulated ship velocity, which
is independent of where the camera looks. The co-moving ship is not distorted as though it
were a passing external object. **GR black-hole lensing is a future milestone in this
checkout**, not a feature already provided by this model. Its eventual integration must
use the same observer contract for all external light and pass its own physics checks.

## Approved demo plan

Mark approved the bounded demo on 4 October 2026. Implementation is in progress; the
Blender reference above remains the measured reference, not a claim of a completed game demo.

1. Perspective bridge view, correct downward occlusion, and whole-ship inspection pullback.
2. One guarded lift from +82 m to the next tier at +57 m, a small walkable landing, and return.
3. Inspected captures, compatibility checks and a laptop review build. Actual laptop performance
   must be measured; Studio performance cannot certify it.

Buildings, forests and unrestricted traversal of all seven tiers follow later. The lift
needs real floor openings and separate active walk surfaces; it must not pass through a
solid floor. Reference camera presets near the rim are diagnostic views and do not certify
that the captain's walking capsule can occupy those points.
