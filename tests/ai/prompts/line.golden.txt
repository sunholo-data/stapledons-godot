- archive.two-clocks: Every journey has two durations, and both are true. The galaxy keeps one clock. A trip to Alpha Centauri at 0.99c takes 4.414 years by that clock: the distance divided by your speed. Your own clock runs slower by the Lorentz factor, which at that speed is 7.0888. Aboard, the same trip takes 0.6227 years, about 227 days. You will step off younger than everyone you left will be when they hear you arrived. Going faster changes the balance. At 0.999999c the galaxy still counts 4.370 years, because nothing can beat light. Aboard, the trip lasts 2.26 days. Nothing about this is a trick of the instruments. Both clocks measure time, each in its own frame. The difference does not come back when you stop. The years at home have passed, and the days aboard are the only days you lived. For comparison, an old-fashioned rocket at a steady 1 g, turning around halfway, would reach a peak speed of 0.9517c. It would take 3.582 years aboard and 6.003 years at home. When you commit to a journey, the Archive shows both clocks and the years left at home. Read them slowly.
