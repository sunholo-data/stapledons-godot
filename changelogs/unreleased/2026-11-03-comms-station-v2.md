### Added
- **Every week from home, on a film** (R3-COMMS-V2 CV1–CV2, design `design_docs/implemented/r3/comms-station-v2.md`). The sim keeps a received-frame log: one record per ship day in which Earth's weeks reached the ship, split where the beam changes. Each record carries the arrival (the ship's galaxy time, proper time, position and the Earth rate) and the send event on the light cone (sent t = arrived t − |p|), plus the ship's own logged state when Earth sent that light. Only light that has arrived is ever logged. Protocol 2.18 sends it as `earth_log` in full states and `earth_log_tail` on change; the game's sessions ask for 2.18. The comms station's new history wall lays the weeks along ship time (sparse flying out, a flood flying home), with static where the light came without Earth's beam and two stamps per picture, Earth date and ship date. Selecting a picture turns the light strip into that week's light-cone diagram on equal axes (light at 45°): where Earth sent it, the light, where it found the ship, where the ship was when it was sent, and ×D. Each arrival is also a ray on the strip.
- **The signal from home as a waterfall** (CV3). Always True: measured with Godot's spectrum analyser from a silent tap that carries the week's sound and Earth's carrier at speed and pitch × D. A teal line marks the Receiver's 400 Hz, and a bracket from the measured carrier to it shows ×D, the Doppler shift. Outside hearing the band goes dark and an arrow says so. Tests: the measured carrier sits at 400 × D within 1 % at the floor of hearing (20 Hz), RS-26, rest and RS-29.
- **The bridge as mission control** (CV4, CV6; Mark 2026-11-03: "can we move the consoles to be underneath the screens?"). A wall of four ambient monitors stands on the bow rail, below the horizon and off the helm's line, each with a grown frame and stand (Blender, `bridge_monitors.glb`). From left to right: Earth's clock against the ship's; the signal from home (the True waterfall with the Receiver's line and ×D); the course; the crew's faces. Each monitor renders only while on screen, every number on it is a binding to a sim field, and it fades like the rim fronds when it would hide the captain. The Voyage, comms and crew consoles now stand under their screens at the rail, facing them; the helm stays central. The walk from the captain's chair to the comms station drops from 17.1 m to 8.5 m. The comms desk has its own brass bezel. Every bow-evaluator metric holds, and the evaluator now counts five stations.
- **Earth's Carrier in the Archive** (CV5): a new entry explaining Earth's 400 Hz tone, which the ship hears at 28.355 Hz flying away at 0.99c and 5,642.7 Hz flying home, why speed takes it out of hearing, and why the receiver corrects it. Its check values are rows RS-41..RS-47, asserted in the physics tests. The course screen now shows the ship time left on the leg (protocol 2.18 `course`). The signal screen draws the measured carrier as a bright trace, so Earth's tone near the floor of hearing reads from across the bridge. The course screen moved to +24°, beside the helm.
- **The screens stand above the rail** (Mark: "the ship railings around the bridge are going through the screens"). Each monitor is raised over the bow rail on a stalk just inside it, and `make monitor-rail-test` checks that no monitor touches the rail or any other prop. Weeks inside a stack can now be opened one by one, each with its own Earth date: a new read-only `earth_weeks` request to the sim. The signal screen draws Earth's carrier from each frame's measurement, accurate to 0.02 %, and does no work while off screen. Earth's tone falls silent below 20 Hz, the floor of human hearing (Mark: "sure 20hz"). New GPU golden checks cover the waterfall and the light-cone diagram. A stack longer than 52 weeks pages, 52 at a time, so its oldest week opens too.
