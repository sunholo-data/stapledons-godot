The wall stops matter. It cannot stop gravity's stretch, because that is not a
thing that travels; it is the shape of space itself.

Near a mass, gravity pulls harder on the near side of the ship than on the far
side. Across our radius of 100 m, that difference is the tide. It falls with
the cube of distance. Measured in units of a black hole's own size, it also
falls with the square of the hole's mass. Small black holes are the most
dangerous.

The nearest known black hole has 9.62 solar masses. At ten horizon radii it
would stretch the bubble with 1.14 × 10⁶ g, and at three horizon radii with
4.21 × 10⁷ g. The ship would be torn apart long before. To feel less than
0.1 g we would have to stay beyond 2,248 horizon radii. A hole of 1,000 solar
masses is gentler but still lethal close in: 105 g at ten horizon radii, and
3,890 g at three.

Sagittarius A*, at the heart of the galaxy, has 4.297 × 10⁶ solar masses. At
three horizon radii its tide across the ship is 2.11 × 10⁻⁴ g. You would not
spill your tea. A tide that mild that close needs a hole of at least
1.97 × 10⁵ solar masses.

That is why the ship goes close only to giants.
