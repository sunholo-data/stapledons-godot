Space between the stars is not empty. Here, in the Local Bubble, there is
about one hydrogen atom in every ten cubic centimetres. At walking pace that
is nothing. At our speeds it is the voyage's real enemy.

Seen from the ship, the gas becomes an oncoming beam. At 0.99c it pushes on
the bow with the force of light from 163 suns. At 0.999999c, it is
1.66 × 10⁶ suns. If the wall absorbed that energy as heat and shone it inward,
no one aboard would survive a single crossing.

It does not. A particle that meets a barrier it cannot climb bounces off it
elastically, like light from a mirror. The wall turns the gas aside without
being heated. What remains is drag: 23.3 N on our sphere at 0.99c. The drive
must work against it. Over the trip to Alpha Centauri that costs
1.37 × 10¹⁷ J, the energy locked in 1.52 kg of matter.

The bounced protons stream ahead of us in a thin, fierce plume. A tiny
fraction of the impacts are not perfectly elastic, and their energy becomes
light. That is the boundary glow: faint, brightest at the bow, and stronger in
denser gas. Crew who keep the Watch read speed and weather in it.
