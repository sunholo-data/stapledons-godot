Your feet press on the deck with exactly the weight they had on Earth. That
weight is a choice the generator makes, not a consequence of the drive.

Inside the bubble the pocket is its own frame, so its motion through space is
not felt. The generator holds a steady 1 g pointing aft. "Down" is toward the
engines; "up" is toward the direction of travel, toward the bridge and the
dome. Earth gravity was chosen for comfort: bones, blood and gardens all
expect it.

The old dream of a starship was different. A rocket accelerating at 1 g would
give its crew Earth weight for free. But physics makes that dream slow,
because felt acceleration does not depend on how much a ship weighs. Reaching
0.99c at a steady 1 g takes 2.56 years of ship time. Reaching 0.999999c takes
7.03 years. Halfway, the ship would turn around and brake for just as long.

The bubble does it differently. A boost to cruise speed can take 10 minutes.
Seen from outside, that is an acceleration of 1.35 × 10⁵ g. Inside, the teacup
on the table does not tremble. And when the drive is silent and the ship
coasts between stars, you still weigh what you weigh.
