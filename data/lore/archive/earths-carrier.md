Under Earth's pictures runs a single steady tone, 400 Hz at home. It is the
carrier the comms receiver locks onto, and the screen above the comms desk
shows it as a bright line climbing or sinking through the waterfall of
everything else Earth sends.

Speed bends that line. Flying away at 0.99c, every wave of Earth's tone has
farther to chase you, and the waves arrive stretched: the tone reaches the
ship at 28.355 Hz, close to the floor of hearing. Turn for home at the same
speed and the waves crowd together, arriving at 5,642.7 Hz.

Human ears hear from 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. At 0.999999c the tone falls to
0.28284 Hz behind you, or rises to 5.6569 × 10⁵ Hz ahead, and no one aboard
could hear it at all. That is why the receiver corrects it, the way the Deep
Space Network corrects the drift of the Voyager probes' signals every day:
the thin line on the screen is where the receiver puts Earth's tone back. The
distance between the two lines is your speed, written in sound.
