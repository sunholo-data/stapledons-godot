A black hole cannot be seen. What you see is the hole it makes in the sky, and
the sky bent around it.

Light passing close is deflected. At 1.5 horizon radii it can circle the
hole: the photon sphere. Rays aimed closer than 2.598 horizon radii from the
centre are captured and never reach you. That missing light is the shadow,
and it is larger than the horizon itself.

From ten horizon radii the shadow has a radius of 14.27°, wider than a hand at
arm's length. From five it is 27.69°. From three it reaches 45.00°, a disc a
right angle across, ringed by bright arcs. At the photon sphere it would reach
90° and fill half the sky.

Stars behind the hole are not hidden. Their light comes around both sides and
arrives as a ring, the Einstein ring, with fainter copies inside it crowding
toward the shadow's edge. Every image is the real sky from behind the hole.

Time runs slower down here too. Hovering at three horizon radii, your clock
runs slow by a factor of 1.225 compared with a distant one, and the starlight
falling in is blueshifted by the same factor of 1.225.
