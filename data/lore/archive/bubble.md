You live inside a sphere of radius 100 m, held by the generator in the spire.
Almost everything about it follows the ordinary laws of physics. One thing does
not, and the Archive will not pretend otherwise.

The invented part is the bubble itself. It does three things. Its wall stops
every particle that has mass, inward and outward, and lets light and neutrinos
pass. Seen from outside, the whole pocket behaves as if it weighs very little,
though never nothing. A pocket that weighed nothing would have to travel at
exactly the speed of light, and your clocks would stop. And inside, the pocket
keeps its own frame: when it accelerates, you do not feel it.

It is called a Higgs bubble, but the name is a label, not an explanation. The
Higgs field gives a proton only about 1% of its mass; the rest is the binding
energy of the strong force. If the field were switched off inside, the
electron would lose its mass and atoms would come apart. So inside the bubble,
physics is normal.

Everything else the Archive tells you about the voyage follows from those
three properties: the drive, the glow, the sky, the danger near black holes.
Ask about any of them.
