Earth is always behind you in time. The newest light from home that reaches
the ship left Earth as many years ago as you are light-years away. At every
port, the news is exactly as old as the distance.

Moving changes how fast that old news arrives. Flying straight away from home
at 0.99c, each hour aboard brings you 0.070888 of an hour of Earth's history.
At 0.999999c it is 7.0711 × 10⁻⁴, and home slows almost to stillness. Turn
for home at that speed and the same history pours in 1414.2 times faster than
you live it.

A message sent after you leave has to chase you. Sent one day behind a ship
at 0.9c, it catches up 10 days after departure. Behind a ship at 0.999999c it
needs 2,737.9 years.

Earth beams only toward plans it has heard. Change course far from home and
your new plan takes years to reach Earth, and Earth's answer takes years more
to reach your new port. Turn at right angles from a port forty light-years
out, thirty light-years across, and the light's path through home is 60 ly
longer than your own. You wait that many years at the new port before Earth's
voice finds you again. Until then, Earth keeps speaking toward the place you
left.
