Look up from the bridge at speed and the stars have gathered overhead. Look
down past the lower decks and the sky has gone dark. Nothing out there has
moved. You have.

Light from every star still arrives, but you are running into it. Its
direction tilts forward, the way rain seems to fall at a slant when you run.
This is aberration. A star that sits exactly abeam at rest appears 60° from
the bow at half the speed of light, and only 25.842° from it at 0.9c.

The light ahead is also squeezed. Waves you run into arrive more often, so
their colours shift toward blue. At 0.9c, light from dead ahead arrives with
its frequency raised by a factor of 4.3589, and light from astern lowered by a
factor of 0.22942. A star's brightness grows as the square of that factor, so
the bow blazes while the stern fades into the infrared.

Crowding, bluing and brightening together make the starbow: a ring of changed
colour around the forward pole. Every star in it is where physics puts it; the
Archive renders no decoration.

When the ship brakes on arrival, the stars slide back to their places, and the
familiar sky returns.
