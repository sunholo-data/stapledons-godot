Bubble ship — geometry and style review

This is the shared review reference for the chosen bubble ship: seven major layers inside a sphere of radius 100 m, with at least 5 m clearance for the lower-floor geometry. Mark chose this layout on 4 October 2026. The bridge uses its actual geometry, unchanged in scale. Buildings, forests and other architecture will occupy the major tiers.

25 m between tier surfaces. The bridge and six lower inhabited tiers, with buildings, forests and other spaces within them.

Camera coordinates and verification

Blender coordinates: +Z up/forward, sphere centre (0, 0, 0). Interior eye (16, −8, 83.7) m; rim eye (19.0513, −9.52565, 83.7) m. Both look outward along the same radial bearing. Eye height is 1.7 m above the bridge. Circle guides are hidden in these captures and do not occlude sightlines.

All physical mesh vertices are inside the 100 m radius sphere. Maximum radius: 98.00 m at the actual needle. The bridge mesh extends to Z 78.30 m at its ramp/underside and Z 98.00 m at its tip; the 44 m dimension describes its main circular deck.

Measurements and centre-ray audit

The chosen geometry​

The bridge floor is at Z +82 m, its main circular deck is 44 m across, and the actual needle reaches Z +98 m. The bubble is 200 m across, centred at the ship origin. Blender +Z is forward/up; −Z is aft/down. There is no occupied level above the bridge. The three thin sphere circles are measurement guides, not a solid hull.

The seven-tier schedule follows a 25 m cadence: bridge, then +57, +32, +7, −18, −43 and −68 m. Revision 2 replaces the old 55% radius rule with a 95 m radius inner spherical envelope. This gives every lower floor at least 5 m clearance to the 100 m bubble, rather than a gap that grows toward the equator. Deck widths are derived from the sphere, not the illustration. The 1 m slab thickness and small edge lip are included in the fit: radius = sqrt(95² − max(Z², (Z−1)²)) − 0.08 metres.

The actual bridge is a deliberate exception in this study: its 22 m radius is much smaller than the bubble cross-section at +82 m. It remains the existing decision platform above the wider tiers; an expanded upper terrace would require a separate geometry choice.

Seven layers are major inhabited tiers, not seven single-storey floors. The 25 m cadence leaves about 24 m between lower floor slabs before architecture and bracing. Buildings can contain smaller storeys, and forests can occupy tall open volumes. The actual bridge ramp and underside locally reduce the space beneath the bridge.

The lower floors are opaque slabs 1 m thick, with a tapered central spire beneath the actual bridge. This is a dimension and occlusion blockout, not a structurally engineered ship. Rooms, roofs, gardens, lifts, bracing and railings on lower levels are absent; they will add occlusion. The 5 m clearance is for floor geometry only: roofs and tree canopies need their own sphere checks at their actual heights. For a point at height h, its horizontal radius must stay below sqrt(100² − h²); using sqrt(95² − h²) also preserves the accepted 5 m buffer. Upper-tier buildings and trees need greater setbacks because the sphere narrows overhead. The imported bridge rails and fronds are present. Invisible game collision meshes are excluded. Bridge fronds have their existing attachments; speculative lower-level growth has not been invented to fill the foreground.

What you can see looking downward​

From the interior eye, 4.11 m inside the edge, the centre ray at 30°, 45° and 60° down hits the bridge floor. A camera cannot see through that floor. With the revised wider floors, at the rim eye, 0.7 m inside the edge, the 30°, 45° and 60° centre rays reach the next deck. The 15° centre ray still clears the modeled ship. Use the angle selector above to compare the full images: a centre-ray result describes only the middle pixel, not everything in the wide field of view.

These are ordinary geometric visibility studies. They do not simulate relativistic aberration or boundary glow. They establish which physical structures are in a camera's view before those optical effects are applied.

How this relates to the game panorama​

The current game composite uses two different projections: the panorama/sky camera looks inward and about 53.6° upward, while the orthographic play layer looks downward at 14°. Those layers are not a single physically coherent camera for arbitrary tilting and panning. The measured views here instead render all ship geometry through one perspective camera. They are a reference for choosing future camera behaviour, not a claim that the current composite already produces these sightlines.

Play, foreground and panorama describe rendering distance and composition, not physical ship storeys. Nearby rails and fronds can form foreground parallax, but every visible member needs a real root and a position in the shared model. The central spire is one continuous structure; it must not be duplicated in a panorama plate.

From the Blender reference to the game​

Recommended approach: keep one master ship assembly in metres, with linked collections for the bridge, six lower tiers, central structure, architecture and vegetation. Export separate game assets from that common design, so all views share positions and proportions.

Walking and close inspectionDetailed bridge or local tier sections, with nearby architecture and gameplay collision
Looking down or pulling back to the whole shipSimpler tier and exterior meshes, with reduced detail for distant buildings and forests
Galaxy mapSmall ship image or simplified mesh, kept legible as a schematic marker

The bridge can be the first detailed section of this assembly. The lower tiers become part of the same coordinate system rather than unrelated scenery. For a continuous zoom and tilt, visible ship geometry needs consistent camera projection. The current painted bridge plate is composed for its fixed isometric view; extending free camera movement will also require suitable materials or baked textures for the other angles.

The current game loads an area's GLB from an area bundle at runtime. The galaxy map currently draws a diamond at the simulated ship position. Neither a detailed whole-ship loader nor a model-based map marker is implemented by this reference-page change.

A smaller transform alone does not make a fully detailed ship inexpensive. Use reduced geometry and materials for distant views, and keep full local detail to the nearby areas. Godot offers mesh LOD and visibility ranges for detail changes. Its automatic LOD is an import-time facility; the current runtime GLB loading path needs deliberately prepared detail levels or a revised import pipeline.

At interstellar map scale, a physically sized 200 m ship is invisible. A map marker should therefore keep a readable screen size, anchored to the true simulated position. A rendered ship icon can share the ship's silhouette and colours without loading its interior. A selected-ship close-up can show the simplified 3D model.
