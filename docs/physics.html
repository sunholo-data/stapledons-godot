The physics

Every visual effect that shows special or general relativity is held to a normative spec, physics/relativity-spec.md in the design repo. Each effect has numbered check values (RS-n, and HB-n for the Higgs bubble). Before a visual merges it needs:

  1. the spec's check values asserted in the CPU physics tests;
  2. a GPU-against-CPU golden case (the shader must put each star where the float64 reference says, to under 0.1 px);
  3. reference renders that a person has actually opened and looked at.

The formulas live first in the AILANG package sunholo/relativity, with tests. GDScript and the shaders mirror the package; they are never the only copy.

Aberration: the sky moves forward​

A moving observer sees every source shifted toward the direction of motion. A source at angle θ from the direction of motion, in the galaxy frame, appears at θ′:

cos θ′ = (cos θ + β) / (1 + β cos θ)

CheckValue
RS-1: a source at 90° seen from 0.9c appears at25.842°
RS-2: a source at 90° seen from 0.5c appears at60°

Straight ahead and straight behind do not move. Everything else crowds toward the bow, so at 0.99c almost the whole visible sky fits in a cone a few tens of degrees wide.

Doppler colour and beaming​

Light from ahead is blueshifted and light from behind is redshifted. For a source seen at apparent angle θ′ the Doppler factor is

D = 1 / (γ (1 − β cos θ′))

(equivalently γ(1 + β cos θ) in galaxy-frame angles). Ahead, D = γ(1+β): √19 ≈ 4.359 at 0.9c (RS-3). Astern it is 0.229 (RS-4). Seen sideways, at θ′ = 90°, D = 1/γ: a sideways star is redshifted and dimmer, not brighter.

  • Colour. A blackbody at temperature T is seen as a blackbody at D·T. Each star is drawn from its own colour temperature (from Gaia photometry, or Hipparcos B−V for bright stars) through a Planck spectrum and the CIE 1931 colour-matching functions.
  • Brightness. I_ν/ν³ is invariant and a point source's solid angle scales by 1/D², so the visual-band flux scales by (Y(D·T)/Y(T))/D², which is D² bolometrically. Extended sources such as the Milky Way keep their solid angle per texel, so their radiance scales as D⁴.

Time dilation: two clocks​

The ship's clock runs slow by γ = 1/√(1 − β²). The journey planner runs in the simulation and matches the closed-form equations to 1e-9:

Journey, Sol → α Centauri (4.37 ly)Earth timeShip time
Cruise at 0.99c (HB-20, HB-21)4.414 yr0.6227 yr (227.4 days)
Cruise at 0.999999c (HB-25, HB-26)4.370 yr2.26 days

Near c the game never computes 1 − β by subtraction, which would lose almost every digit. γ, 1 − β and the rapidity φ = atanh β come from the float64 simulation (γ = cosh φ, γβ = sinh φ).

The forward CMB​

The 2.725 K cosmic microwave background is a blackbody too. Straight ahead it is blueshifted by D = γ(1 + β) = e^φ:

T_fwd = T_CMB · γ(1 + β)

CheckValue
HB-62: forward CMB at 0.99c38.44 K
HB-65: γ where it reaches the Draper point, 798 K (a dull red glow)146.4
HB-67: γ where it reaches 1,500 K (plainly orange)275.2
HB-63: forward CMB at 0.999999c (γ 707)3,853.7 K
HB-68: half-temperature radius from the pole at γ 707, 1/γ0.0810°

So above γ ≈ 150 a glow appears dead ahead that no star explains, and at the cruise cap it outshines every star around it.

A photometric, naked-eye sky​

Scene units are physical: stars carry illuminance in lux and the background luminance in cd/m². The default exposure is a dark-adapted eye, calibrated so that a star at the naked-eye threshold lands on the display's lowest visible level. The measured naked-eye limit is V 6.9, against 6.897 from Crumey's model. The deep-space sky is anchored at 23.5 mag/arcsec² and the Milky Way at 20.8.

When the meter needs it, the eye light-adapts, and the HUD says so. Camera metering, a fixed EV, an exposure bias and a magnitude floor are available as labelled options, never silent fudges.

The real nearby stars​

The sky is drawn from real catalogues, built by AILANG tools from pinned downloads:

SourceWhat it gives
CNS5, the fifth catalogue of nearby starsThe stars within about 25 pc, with Gaia photometry
GCNS, the Gaia catalogue of nearby starsAbout 330,000 stars within 100 pc
HipparcosThe bright naked-eye stars (V < 7) such as Rigel, Canopus and Deneb that the others miss

Together that is 335,157 stars, instanced on the GPU, with positions kept in float64 and rebased around the ship so the direction to a target at a 1,000 AU stand-off holds to 2.4e-8 rad.

Stars in multiple systems are kept together. A companion within 60″ and 2,000 AU of its system's primary, agreeing with it in parallax and proper motion, takes its system's distance instead of its own noisier one: 20,524 companions in all, so Sirius B now sits beside Sirius A at 8.6 ly. The galaxy map draws the 5,687 stars within 25 pc from the same catalogue tiers, with α Centauri A and B as separate, selectable stars.

Behind them is the NOIRLab all-sky Milky Way panorama (E. Slawik / NOIRLab / NSF / AURA, CC BY 4.0). An AILANG pass removes the photo stars that the catalogue draws itself (every one brighter than V 4.5, and 96–99% of those down to V 7), so stars are not counted twice, and fits a colour temperature to each texel so the renderer can Doppler shift the Milky Way as a blackbody.

General relativity: black holes (planned, M3)​

Black holes are where the game's hard-SF promise is most visible, and they are next on the roadmap. The spec already fixes what they must show, with check values:

CheckValue
RS-9: critical impact parameter b_c = (3√3/2) r_s2.598 r_s
RS-10: shadow angular radius at 10 r_s14.27°
RS-11, RS-12: at 5 r_s and 3 r_s27.69°, 45.00°

(The retired Go version drew the shadow at r_s, about 2.6 times too small, and gave aberration the wrong sign. The rebuild exists partly to get these right, and to prove them.)
